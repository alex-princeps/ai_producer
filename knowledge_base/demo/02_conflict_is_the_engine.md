# Conflict Is the Engine, the Hero Is the Fuel

*Demo lecture · methodology · written for this repo, MIT-licensed*

A format is a car. Conflict is the engine. The hero is the fuel. You can buy a beautiful car, but without an engine it is a very expensive bench.

The first question to any project is not "what is it about". It is **"who are we against?"** Against the system, against time, against each other, against the audience's expectations. A cooking show is not about food; it is about a chef against a failing restaurant owner who refuses to admit the kitchen smells.

Three layers of conflict:

- **External**: the task, the rules, the clock, the rival. Easy to design, easy to forget.
- **Interpersonal**: people who want different things in the same room. This is where ratings live.
- **Internal**: the hero against their own fear, pride or history. This is where people cry and share clips.

Casting is conflict design done with humans. You do not cast "interesting people"; you cast **collisions**: the perfectionist and the improviser, the old money and the new money, the believer and the cynic.

Rule of thumb: if you can remove a participant and nothing breaks, that participant was decoration. Decoration is expensive and does not trend.
