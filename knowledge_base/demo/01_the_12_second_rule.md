# The 12-Second Rule

*Demo lecture · methodology · written for this repo, MIT-licensed*

Every twelve seconds something must happen on screen. Not "something interesting". Something. A cut, a new face, a raised voice, a price tag, a door that should not open. The viewer does not decide to leave; the viewer's thumb decides, and the thumb has the attention span of a golden retriever on espresso.

Television learned this the expensive way: minute-by-minute ratings show exactly where people went to the fridge. Short-form video just made the fridge closer. The rule did not change, the unit did: on TV you lose a household, on Reels you lose a swipe.

How to apply it:

1. **Map the timeline.** Write the show as a list of beats with timestamps. Any gap longer than 12 seconds without a beat is a leak.
2. **A beat is a change of state.** Information changes (we learn a secret), emotion changes (calm to anger), stakes change (the prize doubles), or the picture changes (new location). Talking heads explaining the plan are not a beat, they are a leak with good lighting.
3. **Front-load the promise.** The first 3 seconds sell the next 57. Show the end-state or the conflict first, explain later.
4. **Kill your darlings in the edit, not in the script.** The script always looks fine. The rough cut never lies.

Common excuse: "our audience is intelligent, they don't need constant stimulation". Your audience is intelligent enough to open another app.
